## Why this document exists

An LLM judge that has not been calibrated is not a cheap measurement. It is a random number generator with good manners, and its output looks exactly like a real number. The figures below say whether this one is measuring anything, which way it fails, and what its scores mean once its own error is taken out of them.

## What these numbers do not say

- The gold set is labelled by one rater. The intra-rater agreement from the second pass is the check on that, and it is published whatever it says; a second rater from outside work would be better and is sought.
- Sensitivity and specificity are measured on this gold set, on this domain, under this rubric. A judge calibrated on financial-regulator guidance is not thereby calibrated on anything else, and the gate stores the rubric hash with every run so a figure can never be quoted against a rubric it was not measured under.
- A corrected rate is only as good as the correction. Where sensitivity plus specificity is close to 1 the division is close to zero and the interval is wide; that is the arithmetic telling the truth, not a bug to be smoothed over.
